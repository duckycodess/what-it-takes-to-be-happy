# Project brief

Confirmed after the design interview on 8 October 2026. Working title: **What Does It Take to Be Happy?** Content can expand as further group notes arrive.

## Purpose and audience

Create a substantial, independently readable website answering the midterm question: what happiness means, what constitutes it, whether it is achievable in present society, and what changes could enable it. The instructor should be able to assess the complete argument without a spoken presentation. General readers should also be able to follow it.

Begin with recognizable Filipino and student circumstances, then widen to workers, caregivers, different households, and people who prefer solitude. Reflect on the group's standpoint instead of treating its circumstances as universal.

## Working argument

Happiness takes different forms. Small joys count, while security, time, relationships, and freedom shape who can sustain them. This is a working synthesis, not a formula that predicts feelings or erases disagreements.

Preserve the useful tension between Koj's meaningful difficulty, TJ's defense of pleasure and rest without productivity, and Vier's emphasis on agency and inherited definitions. Joy can exist during hardship; that does not make the hardship just. Comfortable circumstances do not guarantee happiness.

## Experience

- Readers advance through short illustrated beats with Next and Back. There is no fixed scene count.
- The approximate reading commitment is 15–30 minutes, to be estimated from actual content. It is not a hard cap that deletes distinct perspectives.
- An unseen narrator introduces people and situations and connects the argument. Original avatars for TJ, Koj, and Vier sometimes speak; names above their heads identify them.
- Generic fictional friends may be introduced conversationally, such as "This is our friend Bob." Their situations explain a point; they are not population evidence or invented group autobiography.
- Scenes may develop across several beats or change entirely. The behavior depends on the point being explored.
- Illustrations show varied emotions, including joy, sadness, frustration, fatigue, and curiosity. Happiness is not represented as constant smiling.
- Optional comparisons, extra examples, and supplementary character thoughts can clarify a scene. Next remains available, and all essential perspectives remain in the main sequence.
- Key concepts, findings, caveats, and inward reflection remain visible. Source details and longer supporting material can expand.
- Provide chapter shortcuts and a complete continuous reading/print view.
- Support desktop and phone layouts by rearranging content rather than shrinking an entire stage into unreadable text.
- Use a warm, conversational tone with lightly playful introductions and more reflective treatment of difficult material.

## Landing and visual direction

Use a large centered question, original stickmen with different emotions around it, and a Begin button. The opening can show everyday snapshots of play, meaningful challenge, and rest, followed by the group's different interpretations. This opening does not constrain later storytelling to one method.

The reference is [The Evolution of Trust](https://ncase.me/trust/): original stickman line art, strong typographic hierarchy, and presentation-like pacing. The identified reference font is **Futura Handwritten**. Use original drawings and compositions. Audio is optional later; the argument must work through text and visuals.

## Assignment requirements

Source: [Instructions tab](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1NL3B3DEtVc3QeblkBwtClMhwEmDhqnkwZByL2hFDyVU/edit?tab=t.0), captured in [the instruction snapshot](notes/2026-10-08-instructions.md).

- Choose one question. The Brainstorm identifies the happiness question as Q2.
- Formats beyond the named examples are allowed with equivalent depth and content. The named comparisons are a video/audio recording of at least six minutes, an essay of at least 1,000 words, or a zine of at least eight pages. The assignment specifies no website word count.
- Include personal ideas, observations, contextualization, examination of assumptions, and published research. Gray literature may add perspectives but cannot be the only evidence.
- Identify sources; no prescribed citation style is required.
- Grading criteria are depth/thoroughness, clear logical argumentation, inward reflection about conclusions, and use of class concepts.
- Submit a digital file to the class Google Drive midterm folder, with member names included in the file or filename.
- Deadline: **9 October 2026, 11:59 PM**. The instruction text does not state a timezone; project planning uses Asia/Manila.

## Delivery and evolving contributions

Vercel is the agreed hosting target. Keep a self-contained offline website file for submission; hosting does not replace that deliverable. Both outputs should come from the same authored content.

Research is shared group material. Any avatar can draw on research another member found, provided citations remain attached to original sources and the spoken position matches what that member endorses. Future repo deltas can change or expand scenes without rebuilding the whole narrative architecture.

Application tooling is an implementation choice, not a settled interview requirement. The architecture specifies behavior and data boundaries without imposing a framework.
